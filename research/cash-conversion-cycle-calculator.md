# Research Log: Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Calculator

## 1. Mathematical Grounding & Formula
The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC), also known as the Net Operating Cycle, measures the amount of time (in days) it takes for a business to convert investments in inventory and other operational resources into cash flows from sales.

- **Formula:**
  $$\text{CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}$$
  Where:
  - **Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO / DSI):**
    $$\text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Average Inventory}}{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}} \times \text{Days in Period}$$
  - **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO):**
    $$\text{DSO} = \frac{\text{Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Total Revenue}} \times \text{Days in Period}$$
  - **Days Payables Outstanding (DPO):**
    $$\text{DPO} = \frac{\text{Accounts Payable}}{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}} \times \text{Days in Period}$$

- **Interpretation:**
  - $\text{CCC} < 0$: Negative working capital cycle (the company collects cash from customers before paying its suppliers, e.g. Amazon, Dell).
  - $0 \le \text{CCC} \le 30$: Excellent working capital velocity.
  - $30 < \text{CCC} \le 60$: Typical corporate operational range.
  - $\text{CCC} > 90$: Substantial working capital locked in the operating cycle.
